# System Design Calculations

Back-of-the-envelope calculations help us **estimate a system’s storage, bandwidth, QPS, and server requirements** quickly, without relying on exact data or a calculator.
Bandwidth is the amount of data a network can transfer per second.

The goal is to estimate the **order of magnitude**, not the exact number.

## 1. Storage Units

For quick calculations, use powers of 10.

| Unit | Approximate size | Power of 10 (bytes) |
| ---- | ---------------- | ------------------- |
| 1 KB | 1,000 bytes      | \\(10^3\\)          |
| 1 MB | 1,000 KB         | \\(10^6\\)          |
| 1 GB | 1,000 MB         | \\(10^9\\)          |
| 1 TB | 1,000 GB         | \\(10^{12}\\)       |
| 1 PB | 1,000 TB         | \\(10^{15}\\)       |
| 1 EB | 1,000 PB         | \\(10^{18}\\)       |

Quick tip: Each step up multiplies the size by \\(10^3\\), or 1,000.

**Quick tip:** Each step up multiplies the size by 1,000 (three zeros).

## 2. Common File Sizes

Memorize these rough estimates for system design interviews.

| Data type | Approximate size |
|---|---:|
| 1 text character | 1 byte (typically) |
| Short text post (300 characters) | 300 bytes |
| Smartphone photo | 4 MB |
| HD video (per minute) | 50 MB |
| 4K video (per minute) | 300 MB |

Actual sizes vary depending on encoding, compression, resolution, and metadata.

## 3. Storage Estimation

**Formula:**

Storage per day = Items created per day × Average size per item

### Example: Social Media Posts

Assume a platform generates:
- 500 million text posts per day.
- Average post size: 300 bytes.
- 10% of posts include an image.
- Average image size: 4 MB.

**Text storage:**

500 million × 300 bytes ≈ **150 GB/day**

**Image storage:**

50 million × 4 MB ≈ **200 TB/day**

**Key insight:** Images require far more storage than text.

### Example: Annual Storage Growth

If a platform stores 4 PB of new photos daily:

- Daily storage: 4 PB
- Annual storage: 4 × 365 ≈ **1.5 EB**
- Five-year storage: 1.5 × 5 ≈ **7.5 EB**

This excludes videos, metadata, backups, and replication.

## 4. Replication and Backups

Production systems often keep multiple copies of data for availability and durability.

- If three total copies are required, multiply the primary storage by 3.
- Example: 7.5 EB × 3 = **22.5 EB** of total stored copies.

**Important:** Replication and backups are different. Replication helps with availability; backups help recover from accidental deletion, corruption, or other failures. Both contribute to storage costs.

## 5. Bandwidth Estimation

Bandwidth measures how much data can be transferred per second.

**Remember:** Bytes measure data size; bits per second measure network speed.

- 1 byte = 8 bits
- 1 MB/s = 8 Mbps
- 1 GB/s = 8 Gbps
- 1 TB/s = 8 Tbps

Common network speeds include 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps, and 10 Gbps.

### Example: Video Streaming

Assume a platform delivers 1 billion hours of video daily, averaging 50 MB per minute.

**Step 1: Convert hours to minutes**

1 billion × 60 = 60 billion minutes

**Step 2: Calculate daily data transfer**

60 billion × 50 MB = 3,000 PB/day

**Step 3: Convert data to bytes per second**

Average bandwidth = Daily data transferred ÷ 86,400 seconds

For easier mental math, round 86,400 to 100,000:

3,000 PB ÷ 100,000 ≈ 30 TB/s

**Step 4: Convert bytes to bits**

30 TB/s × 8 ≈ 240 Tbps

Estimated average bandwidth: **240 Tbps**.

This is a simplified estimate; actual requirements depend on video quality, compression, traffic patterns, and caching.

## 6. Peak Bandwidth

Systems rarely experience uniform traffic throughout the day.

A useful starting assumption is:

Peak traffic ≈ 2–3 × Average traffic

Example:
- Average bandwidth: 100 Gbps
- Estimated peak bandwidth: 200–300 Gbps

Treat this as a planning assumption, not a universal rule. Real traffic patterns determine the required capacity.

## 7. Caching and the 80/20 Rule

Often, a small portion of content generates most of the traffic.

For example, 20% of videos might generate 80% of views.

This makes caching popular content highly effective.

**Cache hit ratio** tells us what percentage of requests are served by the cache.

If the cache hit ratio is 80%:
- 80% of requests are served by the cache.
- 20% reach the origin servers.

A higher cache hit ratio reduces origin-server load and bandwidth requirements. However, it does not eliminate the bandwidth needed to deliver content to users.

## 8. Quick Calculation Tricks

- **Thousands:** \(10^3\)
- **Millions:** \(10^6\)
- **Billions:** \(10^9\)
- **Trillions:** \(10^{12}\)
- Multiply by 1,000: shift three decimal places.
- Multiply by 1 million: shift six decimal places.
- Convert hours to minutes: multiply by 60.
- Convert days to seconds: 86,400 ≈ 100,000.
- Round aggressively to simplify calculations.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Storage:** Daily items × Average item size.
2. **Bandwidth:** Data transferred ÷ Time.
3. **Convert bytes to bits:** Multiply by 8.
4. **Plan for peaks:** Average traffic is not enough.
5. **Account for growth:** Storage accumulates over time.
6. **Include redundancy:** Consider replication and backups separately.
7. **Use caching:** Popular content can be served without repeatedly hitting origin servers.
8. **Focus on magnitude:** A sensible estimate is more useful than false precision.
