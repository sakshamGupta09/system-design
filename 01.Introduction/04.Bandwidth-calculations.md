# Bandwidth Estimations

- How much data can be transferred per second.
- The fundamental unit of bandwidth is bits per second (bps).

| Unit | Meaning | Power of 10 (bps) |
|---|---|---|
| bps | Bits per second | 10⁰ |
| Kbps | Kilobits per second | 10³ |
| Mbps | Megabits per second | 10⁶ |
| Gbps | Gigabits per second | 10⁹ |
| Tbps | Terabits per second | 10¹² |

## Example: Video Streaming

Assume a platform delivers 1 billion hours of video daily, averaging 50 MB per minute.

**Step 1: Convert hours to minutes**

1 billion × 60 = 60 billion minutes

**Step 2: Calculate daily data transfer**

60 billion × 50 MB = 3,000 PB/day

**Step 3: Convert data to bytes per second**

Average bandwidth = Daily data transferred ÷ 86,400 seconds

For easier mental math, round 86,400 to 100,000:

3,000 PB ÷ 100,000 ≈ 30 TB/s

**Step 4: Convert bytes to bits**

30 TB/s × 8 ≈ 240 Tbps

Estimated average bandwidth: **240 Tbps**.

This is a simplified estimate; actual requirements depend on video quality, compression, traffic patterns, and caching.

## 6. Peak Bandwidth

Systems rarely experience uniform traffic throughout the day.

A useful starting assumption is:

Peak traffic ≈ 2–3 × Average traffic

Example:
- Average bandwidth: 100 Gbps
- Estimated peak bandwidth: 200–300 Gbps

Treat this as a planning assumption, not a universal rule. Real traffic patterns determine the required capacity.

## 7. Caching and the 80/20 Rule

Often, a small portion of content generates most of the traffic.

For example, 20% of videos might generate 80% of views.

This makes caching popular content highly effective.

**Cache hit ratio** tells us what percentage of requests are served by the cache.

If the cache hit ratio is 80%:
- 80% of requests are served by the cache.
- 20% reach the origin servers.

A higher cache hit ratio reduces origin-server load and bandwidth requirements. However, it does not eliminate the bandwidth needed to deliver content to users.
